Why ZORKA

Fiber systems versus ZORKA: a difference you can't hide

The anti-drone laser market today is filled with fiber-based solutions packed into twenty-foot containers and promising a great deal, yet performing against real targets far worse than advertised. That gap has created a persistent stereotype: laser weapons look impressive in demonstrations and disappoint in the field.

ZORKA-M is not another fiber system in new packaging. It is a fundamentally different beam physics, built into a system that fits where competitors need an entire container, and works where fiber lasers fall short.

  • 01 The 10.6µm wavelength is absorbed far more efficiently by UAV airframe materials than the 1.07µm wavelength used by fiber lasers — delivering significantly deeper penetration, especially at extended range.
  • 02 The 10.6µm wavelength experiences minimal beam degradation from atmospheric turbulence — unlike fiber lasers, it requires no adaptive optics to compensate.
  • 03 An order of magnitude more compact: competing fiber systems require a twenty-foot container to house supporting equipment — our system is several times smaller.
  • 04 The 10.6µm spectrum is safer for the eyes than the spectrum used by fiber lasers, allowing the system to be deployed within populated areas to protect civilian and government infrastructure.
  • 05 Most systems on the market are fiber-based, with mediocre beam quality and low effectiveness against UAVs at extended range. This is what created the stereotype that laser weapons don't work. ZORKA proves otherwise.

This difference in physics isn't an abstract advantage on paper — it's measurable speed: ZORKA-M physically neutralizes a drone in a matter of seconds from the moment the beam makes contact with the target, faster than most operators can manually react to a threat. This isn't a laser demonstrating potential in a lab. It's a system ready to work against real targets under real conditions, today.
